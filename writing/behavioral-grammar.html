Behavioral grammar: a pipeline through Shakespeare

A seven-stage compression method for extracting structural pattern from language, tested on a single scene, and the finding it produced.

Test scene
The Tempest, Act 1 Scene 2, lines 319–374
Method
Seven-stage compression across three domains
Output
Nine-move behavioral pattern with cross-context validation

Premise

Language is behavior made visible. Behavior, once it has a shape, repeats at every scale. Words and actions are parallel outputs of the same underlying structure, and language inflates problems by making structurally similar situations feel fundamentally different from one another.

Hypothesis under test

If linguistic inflation is stripped from a text, the underlying behavioral structure becomes visible. That structure is recognizable across scales — personal argument, institutional conflict, war — because human cognition runs on the same primitive patterns regardless of context.

Shakespeare was selected as the primary corpus because the language is dense with metaphor and redundancy, the text is stable across time, and the plays already encode structural information through form — rhyme, meter, verse, prose — operating as an independent channel alongside semantic content. This means the corpus is partially pre-compressed by a craftsman, and that compression layer becomes a check against the analytical compression that follows.

Method

Seven stages, each preserving the prior. Three domains held independent until synthesis. Semantic content is the only domain that gets simplified; event structure and formal structure are preserved and recorded only.

Stage Name Purpose
0 Anchor Original text preserved unchanged. Every transformation is measured against this.
0.5 Formal signals Log rhyme, meter, verse/prose, entrances and exits. Preserve only; do not simplify.
1 Literal simplification Rewrite each line in plain modern English. Archaic vocabulary and inverted syntax only.
2 Structural normalization Convert to schema: Speaker, Intent, Target, Action, Conflict. Strip poetry; read behavior.
3 Redundancy collapse Ideas appearing more than once reduced to a single instance.
4 Pattern extraction Identify the class of situation. Name it abstractly enough to apply outside the text.
5 Cross-context mapping Test the pattern against situations outside the text. Preserve load-bearing differences.
6 Minimal form Compress to essential structure. Produce the predictive layer. Check against Stage 0.5.

Scene

Prospero summons Caliban from his rocky confinement. What follows is one of the densest exchanges in the play: a confrontation between a man who holds absolute power and a man who holds only language.

William Shakespeare · The Tempest · Act 1 Scene 2, lines 319–374
Prospero

Thou poisonous slave, got by the devil himself upon thy wicked dam, come forth!

Caliban

As wicked dew as e'er my mother brush'd with raven's feather from unwholesome fen drop on you both! A south-west blow on ye and blister you all o'er!

Prospero

For this, be sure, tonight thou shalt have cramps, side-stitches that shall pen thy breath up; urchins shall, for that vast of night that they may work, all exercise on thee; thou shalt be pinch'd as thick as honeycomb, each pinch more stinging than bees that made 'em.

Caliban

I must eat my dinner. This island's mine, by Sycorax my mother, which thou takest from me. When thou camest first, thou strokedst me and madest much of me, wouldst give me water with berries in't, and teach me how to name the bigger light, and how the less, that burn by day and night: and then I loved thee and show'd thee all the qualities o' the isle, the fresh springs, brine-pits, barren place and fertile: cursed be I that did so! All the charms of Sycorax, toads, beetles, bats, light on you! For I am all the subjects that you have, which first was mine own king: and here you sty me in this hard rock, whiles you do keep from me the rest o' the island.

Prospero

Thou most lying slave, whom stripes may move, not kindness! I have used thee, filth as thou art, with human care, and lodged thee in mine own cell, till thou didst seek to violate the honour of my child.

Caliban

O ho, O ho! Would't had been done! Thou didst prevent me; I had peopled else this isle with Calibans.

Prospero

Abhorred slave, which any print of goodness wilt not take, being capable of all ill! I pitied thee, took pains to make thee speak, taught thee each hour one thing or other: when thou didst not, savage, know thine own meaning, but wouldst gabble like a thing most brutish, I endow'd thy purposes with words that made them known. But thy vile race, though thou didst learn, had that in't which good natures could not abide to be with; therefore wast thou deservedly confined into this rock, who hadst deserved more than a prison.

Caliban

You taught me language; and my profit on't is, I know how to curse. The red plague rid you for learning me your language!

Prospero

Hag-seed, hence! Fetch us in fuel; and be quick, thou'rt best, to answer other business.

Formal signals (Stage 0.5)

Before any word is simplified, the structural layer of the scene is recorded. Three domains are kept independent.

Event structure
Prospero is present for the entire scene; he initiates and closes it. Caliban enters on summons and exits on command — neither arrival nor departure is his own. Miranda is present but silent, invoked as justification yet given no voice to answer. One agent controls entry and exit of the other; the scene cannot end until the dominant agent ends it.
Formal structure
Both characters speak in blank verse throughout. Caliban speaking verse signals education — he was given the master's formal language. No rhyme in the scene; rhyme signals closure in Shakespeare, so its absence confirms the scene does not resolve, it is shut down. Prospero's speeches grow from two to six to thirteen lines — verbal escalation while position remains static.
Semantic content
This is the only domain that gets simplified. Simplification happens here and nowhere else. Holding this line is what keeps the formal signals from being destroyed in the act of trying to reveal them.
Observation before analysis

The formal signals run opposite to expected power display. Caliban becomes more compressed and precise as the scene ends. Prospero becomes more verbose and then abruptly stops arguing. Physical power and linguistic clarity are held by different agents.

The exchanges (Stage 2)

Each exchange converted to schema. No poetry, no rhetoric — the interaction itself. Nine exchanges across two agents.

Exchange 1 · Prospero opens
Summons with insult
Intent
Assert dominance; establish terms of interaction
Action
Combines command with degradation
Conflict
Requires the subordinate's presence and labor but frames the requirement as punishment rather than need.
Exchange 2 · Caliban responds
Curse — symbolic refusal
Intent
Refuse the terms of the summons; return degradation
Action
Uses the only weapon available — language
Conflict
Cannot refuse physically; refuses symbolically through his first words.
Exchange 3 · Prospero responds
Threat of physical pain
Intent
Suppress resistance; demonstrate consequence
Action
Promises bodily punishment for words alone
Conflict
Physical threat answers a symbolic act. The disproportionality is the signal.
Exchange 4 · Caliban shifts register
Counter-claim about legitimacy
Intent
Assert prior claim; reframe the entire relationship
Action
States grievance in full — ownership, betrayal, imprisonment
Conflict
Ignores the threat entirely. Makes a counter-claim about the structure of the situation itself. The scene's central structural move.
Exchange 5 · Prospero responds
Redirect to the worst act
Intent
Invalidate the grievance; reassert moral authority
Action
Counter-accusation — the rape attempt as the justifying event
Conflict
Does not address the ownership claim. The grievance goes unanswered.
Exchange 6 · Caliban responds
Fortification
Intent
Refuse shame; escalate defiance
Action
Confirms and celebrates the act — doubles down
Conflict
Confronted with his worst act, the subordinate does not concede. He hardens.
Exchange 7 · Prospero responds
Justification under pressure
Intent
Establish moral justification for the entire arrangement
Action
Extended argument — pity, education, language, confinement
Conflict
The longest speech in the scene. The length itself signals that the justification is under pressure, and contains an undemonstrated premise: that Caliban is inherently incapable of goodness.
Exchange 8 · Caliban's final statement
The reversal
Intent
Collapse the justification with a single reversal
Action
Turns Prospero's own gift — language — against the act of giving it
Conflict
Does not argue against any of Prospero's claims. Makes the gift itself the grievance.
Exchange 9 · Prospero closes
Exit by command
Intent
End the exchange; restore functional control
Action
Insult and command; no further argument
Conflict
Cannot answer the reversal within the logic of the scene. Exits the argument by issuing an order. The scene terminates; the legitimacy question remains unresolved.
Stage 3 finding

The scene contains exactly two arguments and one power move. Caliban wins the argument. He loses the exchange.

Minimal form (Stage 6)

The scene compressed to nine moves. Predictive within the sequence. Recognizable across personal, institutional, colonial, and geopolitical scales without modification.

Legitimacy conflict under asymmetric power
  1. EstablishAsymmetric reciprocity is established.
  2. InvestThe subordinate invests genuinely.
  3. ConsolidateThe dominant consolidates control.
  4. ClaimThe subordinate asserts a legitimacy claim.
  5. RedirectThe dominant redirects to the subordinate's worst act.
  6. FortifyThe subordinate fortifies (binary: requires audience).
  7. JustifyThe dominant inflates around an undemonstrated premise.
  8. ReverseThe subordinate reframes the gift as grievance.
  9. ExitThe dominant terminates by command.

The pattern terminates. It does not resolve. The exchange ends but the situation persists, and the next iteration follows the same sequence. The predictive layer holds within the sequence, not across material outcomes: a personal subordinate may take decades to reach move 8; a state may take generations; an employee may reach it in a single meeting. What the structure predicts is the order of moves and the conditions under which each becomes available, not the timing or the consequence.

Cross-context mapping (Stage 5)

The pattern tested against four situations outside the text. Load-bearing differences carried into each mapping. The question is not whether the situations resemble Shakespeare, but whether the structure is present without forcing.

Institutional · historical
Colonial administration

A colonial power establishes presence with apparent reciprocity — trade, education, religious instruction. The indigenous population invests genuinely. The colonial power consolidates control. When the prior claim is asserted, the administration redirects to subordinate behavior as justification — savagery, incapacity for self-governance — resting on an undemonstrated premise about inherent incapacity. The subordinate population uses the colonial language and legal frameworks given to them to argue for independence. The dominant power cannot defeat the argument on its own terms and reasserts control through force. Legitimacy remains unresolved; post-colonial conflict persists.

Structural match: complete
Individual · institutional
The workplace betrayal

A senior figure invests in a new employee's development, sharing institutional knowledge and access. The employee invests genuinely and reciprocates with loyalty. The senior figure consolidates extraction while keeping the employee subordinate. When the employee raises a grievance — unfair compensation, blocked promotion, credit taken — the senior figure redirects to a performance issue or behavioral incident. The employee either concedes or hardens. If they harden, the manager exits by asserting institutional authority. The grievance is unresolved; the employee leaves or complies under duress.

Structural match: complete
State · geopolitical
The dominant power and the rising state

A dominant state imposes conditions on a subordinate state. The subordinate state complies and invests strategically. When sovereignty is asserted, the dominant state redirects to the subordinate's worst behavior — human rights record, regional aggression, treaty violation — as substitute for engaging the claim. The subordinate state hardens. The dominant state builds extended justification resting on an undemonstrated premise about civilizational incapacity. The subordinate reframes the institutional tools given to it as evidence that the dominant state has violated its own stated principles. The dominant state exits by sanction or force. Legitimacy unresolved; next conflict cycle begins.

Structural match: complete
Personal · intimate
The relationship of dependence

Two people in a relationship of significant power differential — parent and adult child, or partners with asymmetric financial or social control. The dominant party provided genuine resources early; the subordinate invested genuinely. The dominant party begins citing early provisions as grounds for current compliance. When the subordinate raises a grievance about autonomy or reciprocity, the dominant party redirects to the subordinate's worst behavior. The subordinate hardens. The dominant party builds justification resting on the undemonstrated premise that provision creates permanent obligation. The subordinate reframes the gifts as instruments of control. The dominant party exits through emotional pressure, financial threat, or withdrawal.

Structural match: complete
Theoretical finding

The colonial mapping feels like history. The workplace mapping feels like unfairness. The geopolitical mapping feels like international relations. The personal mapping feels like family. Strip the inflation and the same nine moves are present in the same sequence across all four. This is not a metaphor. This is a structural finding.

Methodological addition: dry and wet outputs

Identified at the end of Session 1, after pipeline completion. The pipeline as built is a defensive instrument — every stage is designed to preserve structural integrity, and it produces convergence because it is engineered to. A theory only tested by methods designed to preserve it has not been fully tested.

The dry output (structural schema) also lacks recognition value. A person reading "subordinate fortifies under accusation when audience present" does not see themselves. The truth is accurate but sterile. Two parallel outputs are therefore proposed at each stage:

Dry (structural) Wet (recognizable)
Subordinate reframes gift as grievance. "The teacher fears the student's first true sentence."
Dominant terminates by command when argument fails. "When the argument is lost, the order is given."
Full nine-move pattern. "What you give becomes what you are owed. What is owed becomes what is taken."

Neither works alone. Dry without wet is sterile. Wet without dry is wisdom literature — carries truth but cannot be tested, compared, or built upon. Together they form a working pair: the dry version is the structural truth, the wet version is the recognition vehicle.

This also enables an independent validation set. If a finite alphabet of human exchange exists, every structural primitive should already have folk wisdom attached to it, because humans have been encoding these patterns in language for as long as they have had language. The dry pipeline produces a structural primitive; proverb and aphorism corpora across cultures can then be searched for wet versions that encode it. Convergence across unrelated cultures becomes evidence for the alphabet. Absence becomes a flag.

Note on method

This project depends on a human–AI working relationship that is documented rather than hidden. The theory is human in origin: the hypothesis about language and behavior, the choice of Shakespeare as controlled corpus, the mid-inquiry observation that formal signals (rhyme, meter) operate as an independent compression layer, and the direction of every stage.

The AI contribution is structural: transformation consistency across stages, cross-referencing against existing theory in cognitive linguistics and systems theory, schema generation, and the processing speed that makes multi-stage pipeline work feasible within a single session rather than across months. Neither side is doing the work alone.

What is original is not the recognition that language inflates problems — cognitive linguistics has established this in various forms — but the operational method for stripping inflation in stages while preserving structural information through three independent domains, and the addition of the dry/wet parallel with cross-cultural folk-wisdom validation.
